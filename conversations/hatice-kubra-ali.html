Hatice Kübra Ali

Agriculture, Chemical Industry & International Trade

Foreign Trade Executive · Alkim Kimya

Career Pathways & Industry Insights

Hatice Kübra Ali’s contribution to Episode 02 explores the chemical industry and the transfer of an agricultural background into international communication and trade.

Illustrative draft answers · Guest review pending
These generic, AI-assisted responses are editorial drafts. They have not been provided or approved by the guest and do not describe verified personal experiences or company practices.

1. On Career Transitions

Özgür: How did your career move from an agricultural background into the chemical industry and international trade, and which experiences helped you navigate that transition?

Draft response: Moving from agriculture into the chemical industry can be understood as applying a scientific foundation in a different business setting. Agricultural training develops familiarity with production systems, technical terminology and the relationship between product characteristics and practical use. Those habits can support work with customers and technical colleagues, while the commercial side requires further learning. For a graduate making this transition, I would prioritise understanding the product portfolio, following an order through its different stages and asking experienced colleagues how the teams work together. The aim is to become someone who can connect a customer's question with accurate information and a dependable next step.

2. On Transferable Skills

Özgür: Which skills from your agricultural education have been most useful in international communication and trade, and which new skills did you need to develop?

Draft response: Analytical thinking, attention to detail and the ability to learn unfamiliar technical material are useful across both fields. Agricultural education also encourages a systems perspective: a decision at one stage can affect several stages that follow. In international trade, that mindset helps connect customer requirements, product information, production planning and delivery expectations. Areas for further development include business English, concise correspondence, commercial terminology and coordination across teams. I would practise turning a complicated technical explanation into a clear message while preserving its meaning. Being comfortable asking for clarification is especially valuable when different people use the same term in different ways.

3. On Industry Insights

Özgür: Which recent developments in the chemical industry have most influenced your work, and how do you communicate technical information to customers or partners with different backgrounds?

Draft response: I would focus on developments that affect customers' practical decisions, such as resource efficiency, traceability and the availability of dependable product information. Their relevance depends on the product and market, so it is useful to ask what has changed and why it matters to the customer. When explaining technical information, I would first establish the customer's application and level of familiarity with the subject. Then I would separate the essential specification from supporting detail, use consistent units and confirm any uncertainty with the relevant technical team. Clear communication means making the information usable without promising performance that the available evidence does not support.

4. On Professional Development

Özgür: What would you advise an agriculture graduate who is considering a career outside traditional agricultural roles, especially in international business or trade?

Draft response: I would start by identifying the skills that can travel with you: scientific reasoning, data interpretation, problem-solving and an understanding of production. Then choose a few roles to explore and learn what their day-to-day work involves. Conversations with professionals, internships and opportunities to observe different departments can make that exploration more concrete. Develop your language skills and practise explaining a technical topic to someone outside your field. In applications, show how a project or practical experience demonstrates a useful skill, rather than relying only on the title of your degree. A career change becomes more manageable when it is approached as a sequence of learning steps.

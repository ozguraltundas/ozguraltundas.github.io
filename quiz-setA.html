Set A – Phenotype & Mapping

Learning Mode Exam Mode

1. What is the observable phenotype of the ms8-1 mutant?

ms8 mutants produce white, non-dehiscent anthers.

2. Why is male sterility useful for hybrid seed production?

Male sterility guarantees cross-pollination.

3. What feature distinguishes fertile from sterile anthers in this study?

Color and pollen presence distinguish phenotype.

4. Why were heterozygous MS8/ms8-1 seeds used for EMS mutagenesis?

Heterozygosity enables allelic discovery.

5. What phenotype were researchers searching for in M1 plants?

Sector sterility indicates new mutant alleles.

6. What method was used to map the ms8 locus?

BSA reveals allele frequency shifts linked to phenotype.

7. What does a peak in the BSA allele frequency plot indicate?

The peak marks linkage to causative gene.

8. On which chromosome was the ms8 interval located?

BSA showed a clear peak on Chr4.

9. What does the absence of fertile pollen indicate?

The ms8 mutation disrupts anther development.

10. Why do sterile sectors appear on otherwise fertile heads?

EMS induces mosaicism in M1 plants.

11. Why does BSA require contrasting bulks?

Contrasting bulks reveal linkage peaks.

12. Why does the fertile bulk show no peak for ms8?

Only the sterile pool enriches causal alleles.

13. What does the mapping interval initially contain?

Initial BSA intervals are large.

14. Why was BTx623 used as the genetic background for mapping?

Reference genome simplifies interpretation.

15. What does allele homozygosity in the sterile bulk indicate?

High homozygosity suggests strong linkage.

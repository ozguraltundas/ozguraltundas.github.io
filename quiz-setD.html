Set D – Methods & Interpretation

Learning Mode Exam Mode

1. Why was EMS chosen as the mutagen?

EMS induces G/C→A/T transitions useful for fine mapping.

2. Why is seed mutagenesis used instead of pollen mutagenesis?

Seed mutagenesis ensures whole-plant mosaicism.

3. What is a major weakness of BSA?

Linked loci appear as one peak.

4. Why does sector sequencing improve allele detection?

Sector sequencing isolates causative variants.

5. Why are CRISPR alleles necessary for causal validation?

CRISPR creates clean alleles confirming causality.

6. Why were multiple CRISPR alleles generated?

Independent alleles strengthen evidence.

7. What is one limitation of EMS mutagenesis?

Background load complicates mapping.

8. Why was BTx623 chosen as the reference background?

Reference genome simplifies mapping.

9. Why are supplemental figures important?

Supplements strengthen conclusions.

10. What broader question does targeted mutagenesis address?

Directly mutating elite lines avoids introgression.

11. Why is sequencing sterile sectors more informative than whole-plant sequencing?

Sector sequencing isolates the mutant allele.

12. What limitation does sector sequencing introduce?

Some sector mutations do not enter the germline.

13. Why do the authors interpret the Q111* stop codon as the functional cause of ms8-2?

Stop codons commonly disrupt transcription factor function.

14. What is a major strength of the “Targeted EMS Mutagenesis” method?

Alleles emerge in the desired genetic background.

15. What supports that ms8 mutations do not affect vegetative growth?

ms8 affects only anther development.

16. What is a key requirement when interpreting IGV alignment tracks?

Depth ensures variant confidence.

17. Why is the bHLH phylogeny important for interpreting MS8 function?

Orthology supports functional conservation.

18. Why is the developmental timing in Fig. 4 essential for interpreting sterility?

Timing establishes cause-and-effect.

19. Why did the authors generate multiple independent CRISPR events?

Independent alleles strengthen causal inference.

20. What does the presence of two linked mutations (P98S + Q111*) indicate?

Cis linkage supports a single mutagenized chromosome.

21. What is a limitation of using BSA alone?

BSA peaks may hide multiple candidates.

22. Why is MS8 function inferred to be conserved across grasses?

Clustering with ZmbHLH122 suggests shared roles.

23. What interpretation does Figure S4 most strongly support?

S4 validates mutation calls.

24. Why is the “failure to complement” logic essential in this paper?

Non-complementation = allelism.

25. What experimental design step ensures EMS concentration consistency?

EMS activity varies with pH and temperature.

26. Why are multiple types of evidence needed (mapping + sequencing + CRISPR)?

Multi-step evidence strengthens conclusions.

27. What role does the tapetum play in pollen development?

Tapetum is essential for viable pollen.

28. Why is targeted mutagenesis valuable in long-generation species?

Somatic sectors shorten discovery time.

29. Why is the abstract considered strong for this paper?

Strong abstracts summarize the full story.

30. What agricultural application makes ms8 valuable for breeders?

Male sterility enables hybridization.
